Where Do You Start?

Which Situation Describes You?

Mexican tax law treats you very differently depending on your residency status. Here are the two most common starting points.

I'm a Non-Resident with Mexican Income

You live outside Mexico but earn money here — renting out a property, consulting, or selling real estate. Your income is taxed under Title V of the Income Tax Law (LISR), usually through withholding, not an annual return like residents file.

I'm Moving to Mexico / Becoming a Resident

You're relocating, working remotely from Mexico, or spending most of the year here. At some point you may become a Mexican tax resident, with different (and sometimes more favorable) rules than Title V.

The Basics

How Mexican Tax Residency Is Determined

Mexico does not simply tax you based on citizenship or immigration status (your visa type). Tax residency is a separate legal question, generally based on these factors:

Permanent Home

If your only permanent home is in Mexico, you are generally considered a Mexican tax resident.

Center of Vital Interests

If you have a home in both countries, residency goes to wherever your main income source or center of professional/economic activity is.

183-Day Presence

Spending more than 183 days in Mexico in a calendar year, combined with Mexican-source income, can also establish residency.

Not Automatic

Having a residente temporal/permanente immigration visa does not by itself make you a tax resident — the two are legally separate.

Non-Residents

Title V (LISR): Withholding on Mexican-Source Income

If you are not a Mexican tax resident, income you earn from Mexican sources is generally subject to a final withholding tax, as follows:

Income TypeWithholdingNotes
Rental of real estate in Mexico25% of gross rentNo deductions by default; you can file a notice to be taxed on a net basis instead
Professional fees / services performed in Mexico25% of grossTax treaties (e.g., with the US or Canada) may reduce this
Sale of Mexican real estate25% of price or 35% of net gainThe notary handles withholding; the 35%-on-gain option requires a Mexican legal representative
Interest from Mexican sources25%**Rate varies by payer/lender type — can be lower in some cases
Salary for work physically performed in MexicoExempt up to ~$125,900 MXN/yr, then tieredThreshold adjusts periodically

Rates shown reflect LISR Título V as published for 2026 and are general reference, not a tax opinion for your specific case. Applicable tax treaties, your country of residence, and how the income is structured can all change the outcome — I review your documents directly before anything is filed.
